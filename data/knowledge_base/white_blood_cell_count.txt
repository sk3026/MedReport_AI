White Blood Cell Count

White blood cells (WBCs) are part of the immune system and help the body respond to infections and other processes.

The white blood cell count measures the number of white blood cells in a given volume of blood.

A higher WBC count can occur with infections, inflammation, stress, certain medications, and other conditions.

A lower WBC count can occur for various reasons, including some infections, medications, nutritional problems, and conditions affecting blood-cell production.

The total WBC count should be interpreted together with the differential count when available, clinical information, and the laboratory reference range.

An abnormal WBC count does not by itself identify a specific disease.

This information is educational and does not constitute a medical diagnosis.
